## Background

Wandering spleen is a rare condition in which absent or lax suspensory ligaments allow the spleen to leave the left upper quadrant and move, with its vascular pedicle, within the abdomen or pelvis **[REF]**. The long pedicle can twist. Torsion may be intermittent and cause recurrent abdominal pain, or acute and cause splenic infarction, and whether the spleen can be saved depends on whether it is still viable at operation. Splenectomy in a child carries a lifelong risk of overwhelming infection **[REF]**, so preservation, with fixation of a viable spleen, is preferred when it is possible.

The published experience consists almost entirely of case reports, small series and reviews of them [1–9]. Delay in diagnosis and treatment is frequently named as a cause of splenic loss, but we are not aware of a systematic analysis of this assumption. The most recent systematic review of paediatric wandering spleen, by Ganarin and colleagues (PROSPERO CRD42018089971), pooled 197 children, 194 of them from English-language reports published between 1990 and 2018. It addressed presentation and surgical technique and did not analyse time to surgery [1].

In our own series of five children treated at one institution, four lost the spleen, which prompted the question of how delay relates to splenic loss. A hand-collected set of 53 published reports, used to pilot the extraction form before the protocol was registered, suggested that the relation may differ depending on whether delay is measured as the duration of the index symptomatic episode or as the total length of the symptom history. This informed the choice of the primary exposure, which was specified before the formal searches were run.

Our objective was to pool individual patient data from published paediatric cases, including Chinese-language reports and reports published since 2018, together with our own series, to examine whether a longer duration of the index symptomatic episode before operation is associated with a non-viable spleen at operation.

Before registration, 53 reports (47 in English and 6 in Chinese) were collected by hand and used to pilot the extraction form (76 unique paediatric patients after removal of duplicate reports). Those pilot data do not enter the analysis: all reports, including the 53 piloted, are screened and extracted afresh according to the protocol.

### Eligibility criteria

We included children younger than 18 years at operation (or at diagnosis, for children managed without operation) with wandering spleen confirmed by imaging or at operation, defined as an abnormally mobile spleen displaced from the left upper quadrant because of absent or lax suspensory ligaments, with or without torsion of the pedicle. Congenital and acquired forms (for example after fundoplication or nephrectomy) were both eligible, with acquired cases forming a subgroup. Individual data on age and, for the primary analysis, on splenic viability at operation had to be reported or supplied by the authors. We excluded torsion of an accessory spleen (including a wandering accessory spleen), torsion of an orthotopic spleen without ectopia, splenosis, and patients whose age was not reported individually and could not be established as under 18 years. Children managed without operation were to be summarised descriptively because they have no operative outcome.

Eligible designs were case reports, case series, cohort studies, letters and reviews that describe original patients, provided that individual data were reported or supplied. Our institutional series of five children (ethics approval 2025R156-E01) was pooled with the published cases. We excluded reviews without new patients (used only for reference checking), conference abstracts and editorials without patient data. Reports in English or Chinese were to be extracted; reports in other languages were to be listed and counted but not extracted.

### Information sources and search

We searched MEDLINE via PubMed, Web of Science Core Collection, Scopus, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang Data and the China Biology Medicine database (CBM, via SinoMed). Strategies combined controlled vocabulary, where it exists, with free-text synonyms for wandering spleen, splenic torsion and splenopexy, with no limits on language, date, age or study design, and were reviewed by a medical librarian before the searches were run. ### Selection of reports

Records were deduplicated with a documented rule-based script (identical DOI; identical title with the same first author and year; similar title with the same first author; same first author, year, volume and first page; and further rules for Chinese-language records and differently written author names). EndNote's duplicate finder was then run on the result as a check, and nothing beyond known distinct publications and patents was flagged. Of 4906 records, 2358 duplicates were removed and 2548 records were screened.

Screening was done in Rayyan with blind mode on and author and journal details hidden. Two reviewers (JS and FP) were to screen titles and abstracts independently against the eligibility criteria, using a written screening guide that applies the criteria without changing them: when in doubt, Maybe; every Exclude carries one of six fixed reasons; records without an abstract are rated Maybe or Exclude only. Before formal screening, the reviewers screened a random calibration sample of 50 of the 2548 records and agreed on 45 of them (90.0%; Cohen's kappa 0.83); all five disagreements were among the 11 records without an abstract. Author and journal details were visible to both reviewers during part of the calibration. According to the protocol, Rayyan's artificial-intelligence functions and its automatic duplicate handling were not to be used. Disagreements in which one reviewer excluded and the other did not were to be resolved by the adjudicator (JY) from an exported list outside Rayyan.

### Data collection

Two reviewers were to extract data independently for every included patient with a pre-specified form (Supplementary File 2) after calibration on 10 reports, and agreement on the primary exposure and the primary outcome was to be reported as Cohen's kappa. Where a report described patients only in aggregate, the corresponding author was to be emailed twice, four weeks apart, to request individual data; otherwise the report would be described narratively. Duplicate reports of the same patients were identified by matching institution, study period, age, sex and clinical details, and the most complete report was used. **[PENDING: actual extraction process, calibration result, kappa, and responses to requests.]**

### Variables

The **primary exposure** was the duration of the index symptomatic episode: the time from the onset of the symptoms of the presentation that led to operation until the operation, in days. Time in hospital before operation during the same admission counted; a planned interval between discharge and a scheduled elective operation did not and was recorded separately as a waiting interval. Rules for converting reported durations are given in Supplementary File 2.

**Secondary exposures** were the total symptom history from the first symptom attributed to wandering spleen; a missed diagnosis, defined as at least one documented earlier healthcare contact at which wandering spleen was not diagnosed or an alternative diagnosis was made, including misread imaging; and a known diagnosis with deferred surgery.

The **primary outcome** was a non-viable spleen at the index operation: total or partial splenectomy performed because of infarction, necrosis or failure to reperfuse after detorsion, as judged at operation or on histology. A viable spleen removed for another reason counted as viable for this outcome. **Additional outcomes** were removal of a viable spleen at the index operation; failure after splenopexy by fixation technique; torsion while a child with a known diagnosis was awaiting surgery; splenic status and length of follow-up; preoperative platelet count and thrombocytosis (above 450 × 10⁹/L); and associated organ involvement and reported post-splenectomy complications.

### Risk of bias

Two reviewers were to assess each report independently with the tool of Murad and colleagues for case reports and case series [13], using the items on selection, ascertainment of exposure, ascertainment of outcome, alternative explanations, adequacy of follow-up and sufficiency of reporting, and omitting challenge–rechallenge and dose–response. No summary score was to be calculated. A report was to be judged adequate for the primary question when the exposure, outcome and reporting items were all satisfied.

### Synthesis

We summarised the characteristics of all included patients with counts and percentages or medians and ranges. The primary analysis was a one-stage logistic regression of a non-viable spleen on log2-transformed index-episode duration, with cluster-robust standard errors by report, adjusted for age (years) and period of publication (before 2000, 2000–2014, 2015 onward); the effect was expressed as an odds ratio per doubling of duration. Degree of torsion and infarction lie on the causal pathway and were not adjusted for. If there were at least 10 events per model degree of freedom, non-linearity was to be examined with a restricted cubic spline with three knots. Secondary analyses used four duration categories (2 days or less, 3–7, 8–30, more than 30 days) with exact 95% confidence intervals and a test for trend, the same model for total symptom history and the binary exposures, and exact confidence intervals for the proportion of children who developed torsion while awaiting surgery.

Missing durations were handled by complete-case analysis, a comparison of patients with and without a reported duration, and bounding analyses that assigned all missing durations first to the shortest and then to the longest category; multiple imputation was not used because duration is unlikely to be missing at random. Pre-specified subgroups were presentation with gastric volvulus or gastric outlet obstruction, congenital versus acquired wandering spleen, and an associated diaphragmatic anomaly. Pre-specified sensitivity analyses excluded our own five patients, restricted the analysis to pedicle torsion confirmed at operation, coded partial splenectomy for partial infarction as salvage, used alternative coding of intermittent or worsening episodes, restricted the analysis to reports judged adequate, excluded Chinese-language reports, excluded reports published before 2000, and used mixed-effects logistic regression with a random intercept for each report. Certainty of evidence was to be described with GRADE principles. Analyses were to be done in R **[PENDING: version]**, with the analysis script fixed before the final dataset was analysed.

## Discussion

**[PENDING: the Discussion cannot be written before the results. Planned structure.]**

- **Principal findings.** State the primary result and its direction as it is, including a null or non-linear result.
- **Comparison with earlier reviews.** Ganarin and colleagues [1] did not analyse time to surgery; relate the findings to the multicentre and pooled experience on splenopexy [5, 7] and to the other reviews [2–4, 6, 8, 9].
- **Interpretation.** The pilot suggested a signal for the duration of the index episode and not for long chronic histories, and in our own series the losses followed index episodes lasting days rather than weeks, in three children after surgery had been deferred for a known diagnosis and in one after a missed diagnosis **[PENDING: check against the case data]**. Discuss whether symptom duration under-measures silent chronic torsion, and the difference between a missed diagnosis and a known diagnosis for which surgery was deferred. Report torsion while awaiting surgery as a separate outcome.
- **Strengths.** Pre-registered protocol with the exposure and outcome fixed before the formal searches; individual patient data; inclusion of Chinese-language reports; separate reporting of viable spleens removed and failures after splenopexy.
- **Limitations.** Evidence from case reports and small series is expected to be of very low certainty, with selective reporting of successful salvage and of favourable timelines. Embase and Google Scholar were not searched and citation searching was incomplete, so relevant reports may have been missed. Reports in languages other than English and Chinese were listed but not extracted. The duration of symptoms is reported inconsistently and may be missing for children whose spleen was saved. Our own series is small and single-centre. **[PENDING: limitations arising from the screening process, once decided.]**
