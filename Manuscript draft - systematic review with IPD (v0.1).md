## Background

Wandering spleen is a rare condition in which absent or lax suspensory ligaments allow the spleen to leave the left upper quadrant and move, with its vascular pedicle, within the abdomen or pelvis **[REF]**. The long pedicle can twist. Torsion may be intermittent and cause recurrent abdominal pain, or acute and cause splenic infarction, and whether the spleen can be saved depends on whether it is still viable at operation. Splenectomy in a child carries a lifelong risk of overwhelming infection **[REF]**, so preservation, with fixation of a viable spleen, is preferred when it is possible.

The published experience consists almost entirely of case reports, small series and reviews of them [1–9]. Delay in diagnosis and treatment is frequently named as a cause of splenic loss, but we are not aware of a systematic analysis of this assumption. The most recent systematic review of paediatric wandering spleen, by Ganarin and colleagues (PROSPERO CRD42018089971), pooled 197 children, 194 of them from English-language reports published between 1990 and 2018. It addressed presentation and surgical technique and did not analyse time to surgery [1].

In our own series of five children treated at one institution, four lost the spleen, which prompted the question of how delay relates to splenic loss. A hand-collected set of 53 published reports, used to pilot the extraction form before the protocol was registered, suggested that the relation may differ depending on whether delay is measured as the duration of the index symptomatic episode or as the total length of the symptom history. This informed the choice of the primary exposure, which was specified before the formal searches were run.

Our objective was to pool individual patient data from published paediatric cases, including Chinese-language reports and reports published since 2018, together with our own series, to examine whether a longer duration of the index symptomatic episode before operation is associated with a non-viable spleen at operation.

Before registration, 53 reports (47 in English and 6 in Chinese) were collected by hand and used to pilot the extraction form (76 unique paediatric patients after removal of duplicate reports). Those pilot data do not enter the analysis: all reports, including the 53 piloted, are screened and extracted afresh according to the protocol.

### Eligibility criteria

We included children younger than 18 years at operation (or at diagnosis, for children managed without operation) with wandering spleen confirmed by imaging or at operation, defined as an abnormally mobile spleen displaced from the left upper quadrant because of absent or lax suspensory ligaments, with or without torsion of the pedicle. Congenital and acquired forms (for example after fundoplication or nephrectomy) were both eligible, with acquired cases forming a subgroup. Individual data on age and, for the primary analysis, on splenic viability at operation had to be reported or supplied by the authors. We excluded torsion of an accessory spleen (including a wandering accessory spleen), torsion of an orthotopic spleen without ectopia, splenosis, and patients whose age was not reported individually and could not be established as under 18 years. Children managed without operation were to be summarised descriptively because they have no operative outcome.

Eligible designs were case reports, case series, cohort studies, letters and reviews that describe original patients, provided that individual data were reported or supplied. Our institutional series of five children (ethics approval 2025R156-E01) was pooled with the published cases. We excluded reviews without new patients (used only for reference checking), conference abstracts and editorials without patient data. Reports in English or Chinese were to be extracted; reports in other languages were to be listed and counted but not extracted.

### Selection of reports

Records were deduplicated with a documented rule-based script (identical DOI; identical title with the same first author and year; similar title with the same first author; same first author, year, volume and first page; and further rules for Chinese-language records and differently written author names). EndNote's duplicate finder was then run on the result as a check, and nothing beyond known distinct publications and patents was flagged. Of 4906 records, 2358 duplicates were removed and 2548 records were screened.

Screening was done in Rayyan with blind mode on and author and journal details hidden. Two reviewers (JS and FP) recorded an Include, Maybe or Exclude decision on the title and abstract of each of the 2548 records, using a written screening guide that applies the eligibility criteria without changing them: when in doubt, Maybe; every Exclude carries one of six fixed reasons (not human, not wandering spleen, adults only, no original patient data, not a formal article, other); records without an abstract are rated Maybe or Exclude only. An Exclude recorded without one of the six reasons was counted as Maybe. Before formal screening, the two reviewers screened a random calibration sample of 50 of the 2548 records and agreed on 45 of them (90.0%; Cohen's kappa 0.83); all five disagreements were among the 11 records without an abstract. Author and journal details were visible to both reviewers during part of the calibration. According to the protocol, Rayyan's artificial-intelligence functions and its automatic duplicate handling were not to be used.

The records to be read in full were chosen with the help of a third reviewer (JY), who judged records on a blinded sheet that showed the title, abstract, year and language but neither reviewer's decision, using the same guide. He judged (1) every record that one reviewer had advanced (Include or Maybe) and the other had excluded; (2) a targeted set of records excluded by both reviewers, namely those whose title or abstract contained a word for wandering spleen or torsion together with a word for a child, and those without an abstract; and (3) a random sample of 60 of the remaining records excluded by both reviewers. Records excluded by both reviewers for being non-human or not a formal article were not rechecked. The rule for extending the check was fixed before the judgements were made: if the third reviewer advanced more than 10% of the targeted records, or at least 2 of the 60 sampled records, he would also judge every other record that either reviewer had excluded as not wandering spleen or adults only. The reports sought in full were those advanced by both reviewers, those advanced by the third reviewer, and, as a deviation described below, 27 further records. Reports published before 2000 were not sought (see Deviations from the protocol), and reports in languages other than English and Chinese were listed and counted but not retrieved.

### Data collection

Two reviewers were to extract data independently for every included patient with a pre-specified form (Additional file 2) after calibration on 10 reports, and agreement on the primary exposure and the primary outcome was to be reported as Cohen's kappa. Where a report described patients only in aggregate, the corresponding author was to be emailed twice, four weeks apart, to request individual data; otherwise the report would be described narratively. Duplicate reports of the same patients were identified by matching institution, study period, age, sex and clinical details, and the most complete report was used. **[PENDING: actual extraction process, calibration result, kappa, and responses to requests.]**

### Variables

The **primary exposure** was the duration of the index symptomatic episode: the time from the onset of the symptoms of the presentation that led to operation until the operation, in days. Time in hospital before operation during the same admission counted; a planned interval between discharge and a scheduled elective operation did not and was recorded separately as a waiting interval. Rules for converting reported durations are given in Additional file 2.

**Secondary exposures** were the total symptom history from the first symptom attributed to wandering spleen; a missed diagnosis, defined as at least one documented earlier healthcare contact at which wandering spleen was not diagnosed or an alternative diagnosis was made, including misread imaging; and a known diagnosis with deferred surgery.

The **primary outcome** was a non-viable spleen at the index operation: total or partial splenectomy performed because of infarction, necrosis or failure to reperfuse after detorsion, as judged at operation or on histology. A viable spleen removed for another reason counted as viable for this outcome. **Additional outcomes** were removal of a viable spleen at the index operation; failure after splenopexy by fixation technique; torsion while a child with a known diagnosis was awaiting surgery; splenic status and length of follow-up; preoperative platelet count and thrombocytosis (above 450 × 10⁹/L); and associated organ involvement and reported post-splenectomy complications.

### Risk of bias

Two reviewers were to assess each report independently with the tool of Murad and colleagues for case reports and case series [13], using the items on selection, ascertainment of exposure, ascertainment of outcome, alternative explanations, adequacy of follow-up and sufficiency of reporting, and omitting challenge–rechallenge and dose–response. No summary score was to be calculated. A report was to be judged adequate for the primary question when the exposure, outcome and reporting items were all satisfied.

### Synthesis

We summarised the characteristics of all included patients with counts and percentages or medians and ranges. The primary analysis was a one-stage logistic regression of a non-viable spleen on log2-transformed index-episode duration, with cluster-robust standard errors by report, adjusted for age (years) and period of publication (before 2000, 2000–2014, 2015 onward) **[PENDING: reports before 2000 were not sought, so the model will use two periods, 2000–2014 and 2015 onward; confirm with the statistician]**; the effect was expressed as an odds ratio per doubling of duration. Degree of torsion and infarction lie on the causal pathway and were not adjusted for. If there were at least 10 events per model degree of freedom, non-linearity was to be examined with a restricted cubic spline with three knots. Secondary analyses used four duration categories (2 days or less, 3–7, 8–30, more than 30 days) with exact 95% confidence intervals and a test for trend, the same model for total symptom history and the binary exposures, and exact confidence intervals for the proportion of children who developed torsion while awaiting surgery.

Missing durations were handled by complete-case analysis, a comparison of patients with and without a reported duration, and bounding analyses that assigned all missing durations first to the shortest and then to the longest category; multiple imputation was not used because duration is unlikely to be missing at random. Pre-specified subgroups were presentation with gastric volvulus or gastric outlet obstruction, congenital versus acquired wandering spleen, and an associated diaphragmatic anomaly. Pre-specified sensitivity analyses excluded our own five patients, restricted the analysis to pedicle torsion confirmed at operation, coded partial splenectomy for partial infarction as salvage, used alternative coding of intermittent or worsening episodes, restricted the analysis to reports judged adequate, excluded Chinese-language reports, excluded reports published before 2000, and used mixed-effects logistic regression with a random intercept for each report. Certainty of evidence was to be described with GRADE principles. Analyses were to be done in R **[PENDING: version]**, with the analysis script fixed before the final dataset was analysed.

### Institutional series

We retrospectively reviewed the records of all children treated surgically for wandering spleen at Wuhan Children's Hospital between 1 May 2015 and 1 May 2026. The definition of wandering spleen was that of the review. Children in whom the diagnosis was made but who never underwent splenic surgery were not included, so the series describes only children who came to operation, and this differs from the review, in which children managed without operation were to be summarised descriptively. **[PENDING: how the children were identified in the hospital records (operation register, diagnosis codes or another method) and whether any child who met these criteria was left out.]** Five children were identified.

Demographic characteristics, presenting symptoms and their duration, physical findings, laboratory results (platelet count at every encounter), imaging findings, operative details and the postoperative course were taken from the medical records. Each hospital encounter was recorded separately, so that the interval between the first presentation and splenic surgery could be determined. Follow-up was by outpatient review and telephone interview, with abdominal ultrasonography or contrast-enhanced CT after operation to assess the position and perfusion of the spleen. For the pooled analysis the five children were coded with the same variables and conversion rules as the published cases (Additional file 2) and entered as one report. **[PENDING: who coded the children, whether a second author checked the coding against the records, and whether the five children are one cluster in the cluster-robust analysis (to be confirmed with the statistician).]**

The study was approved by the ethics committee of Wuhan Children's Hospital (2025R156-E01), and the approval was supplemented to cover the fifth patient. Because the data were collected retrospectively and no identifiable information is reported, the committee waived the requirement for individual informed consent to the review of the records. **[PENDING: confirm written consent for publication of clinical details and images from the parents of all five children, and whether the supplementary approval carries its own number.]**

### Deviations from the protocol

- Embase was not searched, because access was lost after the Emtree check on 29 September 2026 and could not be restored.
- Google Scholar was not searched, because Google blocked the search as unusual traffic.
- Citation searching was not completed: the lists of reports citing Ganarin and colleagues in Web of Science and Scopus were viewed (13 documents each) but not exported or screened, and the reference lists of earlier reviews were not checked. **[PENDING: whether the reference lists of included reports were checked.]**
- Conflicts between the two reviewers at the title and abstract stage were not settled by discussion between them but by the third reviewer, judging blind to both decisions, and the third reviewer also checked records that both reviewers had excluded (targeted set, random sample and the extension rule described under Selection of reports). The protocol provided for discussion or a third reviewer; the blinded sampling and the extension rule were added to the procedure before the third reviewer began.
- Reports published before 2000 were not sought. This date rule was decided on 17 October 2026, after the title and abstract stage and before any full text was retrieved or any data were extracted; the main reason was feasibility, because most reports of that period have no abstract and their full texts are hard to obtain. The registered eligibility criteria had no date limit, and the registered sensitivity analysis that excluded reports published before 2000 therefore no longer applies. The hand-collected pilot set contained no report earlier than 1998, so the effect of the rule on the results is unknown.
- Twenty-seven records that the third reviewer excluded as reporting no original patient data, and that FP had advanced, were also sought in full, because a judgement about study design made on a short or truncated abstract can hide a case series.

### Institutional series

Five children, two girls and three boys, were operated on for wandering spleen during the study period. They were aged 2.75 to 13.08 years at their first presentation and together accounted for ten hospital encounters. Abdominal pain, alone or with vomiting or distension, was the presenting complaint at every encounter, and ultrasonography or CT established the diagnosis before operation in all five. Table 4 shows the course of each child and how the children were coded for the pooled analysis.

**Course before the index operation.** In Cases 1, 2 and 3 the diagnosis was made at the first presentation and operation was advised, but the families declined and chose conservative management with fluid resuscitation, gastrointestinal decompression and postural measures. Symptoms settled each time, after 5 to 7 days, and the children were discharged. All three were readmitted with recurrent pain and had their first splenic operation 16, 19 and 62 months after the first presentation (mean 32 months); Case 3 attended four times over more than five years. Case 5 had been admitted to another hospital one week before presentation and treated there as acute gastritis. Case 4 was operated on at his first presentation, because of a coexisting gastric perforation.

**Index episode and spleen at operation.** At the index operation the index episode had lasted 1 day in Case 1, 1 day in Case 2, 9 days in Case 3, 2 hours in Case 4 and about 21 hours in Case 5 (18 hours of continuous periumbilical pain before presentation and 2.5 hours from presentation to operation). For Cases 1, 2 and 4 the time from presentation to operation was not available when the children were coded, so none was added (rule 9) and these durations may be underestimates. **[PENDING: take the exact times of onset and operation from the records; where both are known the interval is calculated exactly (rule 8).]** The spleen was non-viable at the index operation in four children. Cases 1 and 2 had pedicle torsion with infarction on CT and underwent total splenectomy, by laparotomy and by laparoscopy converted to laparotomy respectively; their preoperative platelet counts were 428 and 525 × 10⁹/L. In Case 5 the pedicle was twisted through about 720° and the spleen was congested, dark purple and did not reperfuse after detorsion; the platelet count was normal, the white cell count 24.20 × 10⁹/L and the C-reactive protein 117 mg/L. Non-contrast CT had shown an enlarged, heterogeneous spleen in the anterior lower abdomen and pelvis, a whirl sign formed by the spiralling splenic vessels, perisplenic fat stranding, splenic vessels encircling the splenic flexure of the colon, and free fluid. Total splenectomy with separate double ligation of the splenic artery took 81 minutes, with a blood loss of 10 mL.

**Case 3.** At her third attendance CT showed a wandering spleen measuring 17.3 × 7.6 cm with partial infarction. At laparoscopy the infarcted parenchyma was resected and a subperitoneal pouch was made, but the spleen was too large to fit in it. The upper pole was therefore sutured to the diaphragm and the lower pole to the lateral peritoneum with 5-0 silk, with Hem-o-lok clips added. CT one week later showed a normally sited spleen. One month later she was readmitted with abdominal pain, and CT showed recurrent migration of the spleen (16.6 × 7.6 cm) with anteromedial rotation of the body and tail of the pancreas. Total splenectomy followed, and the resected spleen was cut into 3-cm slices and implanted in the greater omentum. Under the protocol's primary definition the spleen was non-viable at the index operation, because a partial splenectomy was done for infarction; in the sensitivity analysis that codes partial splenectomy for partial infarction as salvage it counts as viable, and the later total splenectomy is then reported as failure after splenopexy. **[PENDING: the authors to confirm this coding, to state whether the spleen removed at the second operation was viable, and to check the duration against the chart: the nine days were intermittent pain, and the duration of any worsening is needed for the alternative coding in rule 6.]**

**Case 4 and the reinforced two-pole fixation.** Case 4 presented with two hours of abdominal distension. Non-contrast CT showed marked gastric distension with a wandering spleen and pedicle torsion, and contrast-enhanced CT 1.5 hours later showed free intraperitoneal and pelvic fluid, gastric volvulus and a gastrointestinal perforation; the spleen measured 8.4 × 3.7 cm and enhanced uniformly. At emergency laparotomy the stomach had rotated about its mesenteric axis; after reduction a 2-cm perforation on the posterior wall of the antrum was repaired. The spleen lay in the left mid-abdomen, held only by its pedicle, and was viable. It was returned to the left upper quadrant and fixed at both poles (Figure 2). Absorbable haemostatic gauze was laid on the visceral and diaphragmatic surfaces of the upper pole. A 2-0 non-absorbable silk suture was passed through the gauze on the visceral surface and the full thickness of the splenic parenchyma, out through the gauze on the diaphragmatic surface, through the diaphragmatic muscle, back through the gauze and the full thickness of the parenchyma, and tied on the visceral surface. The lower pole was fixed to the left lateral abdominal wall in the same way. He recovered without incident, and CT at 13 months showed a normally positioned spleen; he had no symptoms. **[PENDING: Figure 2, the schematic of the technique, at the journal's resolution; follow-up of Cases 4 and 5 to be updated at submission.]**

**After operation.** All four children who had a total splenectomy developed thrombocytosis, which reached 918 × 10⁹/L on the ninth postoperative day in Case 5, and the count returned to normal on oral dipyridamole in every case. None had overwhelming post-splenectomy infection, thromboembolism or pulmonary hypertension during follow-up. Case 5 was well 5 months after operation. **[PENDING: length of follow-up of Cases 1, 2 and 3, and whether vaccination and antibiotic prophylaxis after splenectomy are documented.]**

## Discussion

**[PENDING: the Discussion cannot be written before the results. Planned structure.]**

- **Principal findings.** State the primary result and its direction as it is, including a null or non-linear result.
- **Comparison with earlier reviews.** Ganarin and colleagues [1] did not analyse time to surgery; relate the findings to the multicentre and pooled experience on splenopexy [5, 7] and to the other reviews [2–4, 6, 8, 9].
- **Interpretation.** The pilot suggested a signal for the duration of the index episode and not for long chronic histories, and in our own series the losses followed index episodes lasting days rather than weeks, in three children after surgery had been deferred for a known diagnosis and in one after a missed diagnosis (checked against Table 4: the index episodes lasted 0.9, 1, 1 and 9 days). Discuss whether symptom duration under-measures silent chronic torsion, and the difference between a missed diagnosis and a known diagnosis for which surgery was deferred. Report torsion while awaiting surgery as a separate outcome.
- **Strengths.** Pre-registered protocol with the exposure and outcome fixed before the formal searches; individual patient data; inclusion of Chinese-language reports; separate reporting of viable spleens removed and failures after splenopexy.
- **Limitations.** Evidence from case reports and small series is expected to be of very low certainty, with selective reporting of successful salvage and of favourable timelines. Embase and Google Scholar were not searched and citation searching was incomplete, so relevant reports may have been missed. Reports in languages other than English and Chinese were listed but not extracted, and reports published before 2000 were not sought, so cases described in earlier decades, including possible long histories, are not represented. The duration of symptoms is reported inconsistently and may be missing for children whose spleen was saved. Our own series is small and single-centre and includes only children who came to operation. Records excluded by both reviewers were checked only in part: the random sample of 60 contained no record advanced by the third reviewer, but 12 of the 616 records judged after the extension rule were advanced, so some eligible reports may have been missed at the title and abstract stage. Conflicts between the two reviewers were settled by a single third reviewer.
