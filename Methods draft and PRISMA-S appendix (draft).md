## 2. Methods（英文，论文用）

### Study design and registration

We carried out a systematic review of individual patient data (IPD) extracted from published reports of wandering spleen in children, pooled with a single-centre case series. The review was registered in PROSPERO (CRD420261520329) on 28 September 2026. The protocol was revised four times after registration; none of the revisions changed the eligibility criteria, exposures, outcomes or analysis plan: version 1.1 (28 September 2026) revised the search strategies after review by a medical librarian; version 1.2 (4 October 2026) added the as-run details of the searches and of deduplication; version 1.3 (7 October 2026) added the screening procedure as set up; and version 1.4 (11 October 2026) corrected the stage of review recorded at that time, because formal screening had already started. `[PENDING: the v1.4 amendment has not yet been submitted to PROSPERO; update this sentence with the date it is submitted and the final version number.]` The review is reported according to PRISMA 2020 [3], PRISMA-IPD [4] and, for the searches, PRISMA-S [13]; the checklists are provided as supplementary files `[PENDING]`.

The research question was set in the registered protocol, before screening and data extraction began: in children younger than 18 years who undergo surgery for wandering spleen, is a longer duration of the index symptomatic episode before operation associated with a non-viable spleen at operation? Before the protocol was written, we extracted data from 53 reports collected by hand (a pilot, not a systematic search). The pilot informed the choice of the primary exposure and is declared in the registration.

### Own case series

The case series comprises five children treated for wandering spleen at Wuhan Children's Hospital (Wuhan Maternal and Child Healthcare Hospital), Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology, China. The study was approved by the hospital's ethics committee (registration number 2025R156-E01), with supplementary approval covering the fifth patient `[PENDING: confirm whether the supplementary approval has its own number]`. `[PENDING: state that written informed consent for publication of clinical details and images was obtained from the parents of all five children — not recorded in the project notes; confirm before writing this sentence.]` These five children were extracted with the same form as the published patients and analysed together with them; a sensitivity analysis excludes them.

### Eligibility criteria

Eligible patients were younger than 18 years at operation (or at diagnosis, for children managed without operation), with a wandering spleen confirmed by imaging and/or at operation. We defined a wandering spleen as an abnormally mobile spleen displaced from the left upper quadrant because of absent or lax suspensory ligaments, with or without torsion of the vascular pedicle. Congenital and acquired forms (for example after fundoplication or nephrectomy) were both eligible, and acquired cases form a subgroup. Individual data on age and, for the primary analysis, on splenic viability at operation had to be reported, or be supplied by the authors. We excluded torsion of an accessory spleen (including a wandering accessory spleen), torsion of an orthotopic spleen without ectopia, splenosis, and patients whose age was not reported individually and could not be established as younger than 18 years. Children managed without operation were summarised descriptively; they have no operative outcome and did not enter the primary analysis.

Eligible reports were case reports, case series, cohort studies, letters and reviews that describe original patients, provided that individual patient data were reported or supplied. Reviews without new patients (used only for checking reference lists), conference abstracts and editorials without patient data were excluded. Reports with full text in English or Chinese were extracted; reports in other languages were listed and counted but not extracted. Any clinical setting in any country was eligible.

### Information sources and search

We searched MEDLINE (via PubMed), Web of Science Core Collection, Scopus, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang Data and the China Biology Medicine database (CBM, via SinoMed). The searches combined controlled vocabulary, where it exists, with free-text synonyms for wandering spleen, splenic torsion and splenopexy, and had no language, date, age or study-design limits. A medical librarian reviewed the strategies before the formal searches, and the PubMed strategy was test-run on 28 September 2026; the review led to the replacement of eight phrases that are not in the PubMed phrase index by proximity searches, to additional synonyms, and to harmonised term lists across databases. The searches were run by one author (JS) between 28 and 29 September 2026 and returned 4,906 records: 1,263 from PubMed, 750 from Web of Science, 1,563 from Scopus, 486 from CNKI, 423 from Wanfang and 421 from CBM. For every database, the number of records exported equalled the number of hits. The full strategies as run, the platforms, the dates and the limits are given in Supplementary Appendix A (summarised in Table S1 below). Coverage in Web of Science is limited by the institutional licence (eight indexes, each from the year of its start; Table S1), so it does not extend to the inception of the database. `[PENDING: the update search — see Updates below.]`

### Deduplication

Records were deduplicated with a documented rule-based script (in order: identical DOI; identical title with the same first author and year; the same title written in another script; similar title with the same first author and year; the same first author, year, volume and first page; the same title, volume and pages; Chinese title with the same year, journal and pages; and the same title, year, journal and volume), and by two manual merges decided after reading the remaining pairs with identical titles (52 such pairs were reviewed and kept apart, being different publications, patents or animal studies). In total 2,358 duplicates were removed (1,293 by DOI, 880 by title, first author and year, and 185 by the remaining rules and the two manual merges), leaving 2,548 records to screen. As a check, EndNote's duplicate finder (default criteria) was run on an intermediate version of 2,550 records; it flagged 32 records, all of them in groups of distinct publications by the same authors or unrelated patents. A looser check by script (identical title and year) found two further duplicates that differed only in the way the authors' names were written; they were merged by the last rule. Two possible duplicate publications of the same patient (one pair of Chinese-language reports and one pair of German-language reports) are to be checked at full text. The script and its output are in the project repository `[PENDING: give the repository or archive address that will be public]`.

### Selection of reports

Titles and abstracts were screened in Rayyan by two reviewers independently (JS and FP), with Rayyan's blind mode on until both had finished the stage, author and journal details hidden, and Rayyan's artificial-intelligence functions, automatic duplicate detection and automatic duplicate resolution not used. Before formal screening, both reviewers screened a random calibration set of 50 of the 2,548 records (random seed 20261004) and discussed the discrepancies: they agreed on 45 of 50 records (90.0%; Cohen's kappa 0.83), and all five disagreements were among the 11 records without an abstract. In the calibration, author and journal details were visible to both reviewers during part of the exercise. A written screening guide, revised after the calibration, applied the eligibility criteria without changing them; its main rules were that Maybe was chosen when in doubt, that every exclusion at this stage carried one of six reasons (not human; not wandering spleen; adults only; no original patient data; not a formal article; other), and that records without an abstract were judged on the title alone. Where one reviewer excluded a record and the other included it or marked it Maybe, the reviewers discussed it and, failing agreement, a third author (JY) decided. Records marked Include or Maybe by either reviewer, and not excluded after discussion, went to full-text assessment.

Formal screening started before the amendment that recorded it: the first reviewer recorded four formal decisions on 7 October 2026 in order to create four of the six exclusion reasons in Rayyan (a reason can only be created by applying it to a record), and the second reviewer screened those four records independently like all others; the second reviewer's first formal decisions were recorded between 7 and 11 October 2026. The written rules had been confirmed by both reviewers and the adjudicator on 7 October 2026, before the second reviewer's first formal decision. `[PENDING: replace by the exact dates and the numbers of records screened by each reviewer from the Rayyan decision log when the stage is complete.]`

### Individual patient data from authors

Where a report described several children only in aggregate, the corresponding author was e-mailed twice, four weeks apart, to request de-identified individual data on the items of the extraction form (no names, hospital numbers, dates of birth or calendar dates). `[PENDING: after the second e-mail, authors were given a further four weeks to reply — full-text guide decision D6, not yet approved.]` Reports for which no individual data were supplied were described narratively and did not enter the pooled analysis. `[PENDING: number of requests, replies and data sets received; what was received.]`

### Data extraction

Two reviewers independently extracted data for every included patient with a predefined form (Supplementary Appendix C), after calibration on 10 reports. For each patient we recorded demographic and clinical characteristics, associated conditions, the timeline of symptoms and earlier healthcare contacts, investigations (including the preoperative platelet count), the operation, the viability of the spleen and its basis, and follow-up; the page, table or figure from which key items were taken was noted. An item that was not reported was coded NR. Reported durations were converted to days by nine predefined rules (for example: hours divided by 24; weeks times 7, months times 30.4, years times 365; ranges take the midpoint; imprecise terms such as "several days" are coded as missing on the continuous scale and assigned a category only if every plausible reading falls in the same category; when both dates are given the interval is calculated exactly). Disagreements were resolved by discussion `[PENDING: the protocol does not name a person to settle disagreements in extraction; confirm who decides, e.g. JY as in screening]`. Agreement between the extractors on the primary exposure and the primary outcome was assessed with Cohen's kappa `[PENDING: how a continuous exposure is compared was not specified in the protocol; the comparison script (tools/compare_extractions.py) reports kappa on the four duration categories plus missing, and on the outcome, and shows the days both exactly and within a tolerance — to be confirmed and stated here]`. Duplicate reports of the same patient were identified by matching institution, study period, age, sex and clinical details; the most complete report was used and the others were linked to it.

### Exposures and outcomes

The primary exposure was the duration of the index symptomatic episode: the time from the onset of the symptoms of the presentation that led to operation until the operation, in days. Time spent in hospital before operation during the same admission was included; a planned interval between discharge and a scheduled elective operation was not, and was recorded separately as a waiting interval. Secondary exposures were the total symptom history, a missed diagnosis at an earlier healthcare contact, and a known diagnosis with deferred surgery. The primary outcome was a non-viable spleen at the index operation: total splenectomy, or partial splenectomy, performed because of infarction, necrosis or failure to reperfuse after detorsion, as judged at operation and/or on histology. A spleen that was viable at operation but removed for another reason counted as viable for this outcome. Additional outcomes were removal of a viable spleen, failure after splenopexy by fixation technique, torsion while awaiting surgery, splenic status and follow-up length, preoperative platelet count and thrombocytosis (above 450 × 10⁹/L), associated organ involvement, and post-splenectomy complications.

### Risk of bias

Two reviewers independently assessed each included report with the tool of Murad et al. for case reports and case series [2], using the items on selection, ascertainment of exposure (whether the timing of symptom onset and of operation is stated precisely), ascertainment of outcome (whether splenic viability is stated, with its basis), alternative explanations, adequacy of follow-up, and sufficiency of reporting; the items on challenge–rechallenge and dose–response do not apply and were omitted. No summary score was calculated; a report was judged adequate for the primary question when the exposure, outcome and reporting items were all satisfied. `[PENDING: assessment not started.]`

### Synthesis

Characteristics of all patients are summarised as counts and percentages or medians and ranges. The primary analysis was a one-stage logistic regression of a non-viable spleen on log2-transformed index-episode duration (odds ratio per doubling, with 95% confidence interval), with cluster-robust standard errors by report, adjusted for age (years) and period of publication (before 2000, 2000–2014, 2015 onward). Degree of torsion and of infarction lie on the causal pathway and were not adjusted for. If the number of events allowed at least 10 events per model degree of freedom, non-linearity was to be examined with a restricted cubic spline with three knots. Secondary analyses used the duration in four categories (2 days or less, 3–7, 8–30, more than 30 days) with exact 95% confidence intervals and a test for trend, and the same model for total symptom history and the binary secondary exposures; the proportion of children with a known diagnosis who developed torsion while awaiting surgery is given with an exact 95% confidence interval, and failures after splenopexy are tabulated by fixation technique. The primary analysis used complete cases. Patients with and without a reported duration were compared, and bounding analyses assigned all missing durations first to the shortest and then to the longest category; multiple imputation was not used, because duration is unlikely to be missing at random (reports of successful salvage may omit the timeline). Outcomes were compared between single case reports and series of three or more consecutive patients as a check for selective reporting. Prespecified subgroups were wandering spleen presenting with gastric volvulus or gastric outlet obstruction, congenital versus acquired wandering spleen, and wandering spleen with versus without a diaphragmatic anomaly. Eight sensitivity analyses were prespecified: excluding our own five patients; restricting to patients with pedicle torsion confirmed at operation; coding partial splenectomy for partial infarction as salvage; the alternative coding of intermittent or worsening episodes; restricting to reports judged adequate in the risk-of-bias assessment; excluding Chinese-language reports; excluding reports published before 2000; and a mixed-effects logistic regression with a random intercept for each report. Certainty of the evidence is described with GRADE principles, and is expected to be very low because it rests on case reports and series. Analyses were done in R `[PENDING: version]`; the analysis script `[PENDING: was frozen before the final dataset was analysed — state the date and where it is kept]`.
