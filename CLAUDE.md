## Self-review and fixes applied (21 Sep 2026)
Claude reviewed both EJP versions as a strict peer reviewer against 9 criteria (novelty, title/abstract/body/conclusion consistency, design and inclusion criteria, statistics, results-vs-conclusions, overclaiming/causal misuse, table-figure-text consistency, academic writing norms, rejection risks) and classified findings as Major/General/Language. The author asked for the 4 Major (must-fix) text-level items to be applied to **both** `EJP original article.docx` and `EJP brief report.docx`:

1. **Causal language softened throughout** ("delay determines splenic loss" / "tracked delay" / "the determinant of" / "lost to delay" → "was associated with delay") — title, abstract, Discussion opening line, and full Conclusion in both versions. The neutral research-question use of "determines" in the Introduction ("what determines whether the spleen is saved") was left as-is — it poses a question, it doesn't assert an answer.
2. **Limitations paragraph now explicitly names the causal-inference limitation**: the cohort only includes children who ultimately had surgery (children diagnosed but never operated on are not captured), n=5 with no statistical comparison, so confounding cannot be excluded — this was previously entirely missing; the old Limitations paragraph only discussed the technique's limitations (n=1, single-centre), never the delay-causation claim's.
3. **Removed the unsupported "adds little operative time" / "It is quick" claim** — no operative-time data exists in the manuscript for the technique itself (only Case 5's splenectomy has a timed value), so this was an unevidenced assertion.
2/3 combined also fixed **the internal contradiction** where the Discussion asserted the technique is "in principle independent of splenic size" and then, two sentences later (Original Article) / next paragraph (Brief Report), admitted it is completely untested in the giant spleen that motivated it. The size-independence claim is now hedged at first mention with a forward pointer ("... although this remains to be confirmed (see below)").

New title (both versions): **"Wandering spleen in children: splenic loss is associated with delay rather than technique, and a reinforced two-pole splenopexy"**.

## Title — final wording (21 Sep 2026)
Author asked whether the comma before "and a reinforced two-pole splenopexy" could be dropped, or whether a better title existed. Analysis: unlike the earlier round (where "and" joined two parallel objects of "determines" and the comma was safely droppable), in this title "and" sits right after "rather than technique" — dropping the comma would make "rather than technique and a reinforced two-pole splenopexy" read as a single compound object of "rather than," reversing the meaning (implying the technique is also being contrasted away, not separately introduced). So the comma could not simply be removed.

**Final title (both EJP versions): "Wandering spleen in children: splenic loss is associated with delay rather than technique, and a reinforced two-pole splenopexy is described."** Added "is described" so the second half is a full independent clause, making the comma a standard clause-joining comma rather than a fragile disambiguator, while keeping the technique named per the author's original requirement. Applied to both `EJP original article.docx` and `EJP brief report.docx`, rebuilt, schema-validated, committed.
